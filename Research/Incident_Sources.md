# Incident Sources

## Selected Incident

**Incident:** Twitter July 2020 Cybersecurity Incident  
**Incident Date:** 15 July 2020  
**Organization:** Twitter  
**Attack Type:** Social Engineering / Vishing / Phishing

---

## Source 1 — New York Department of Financial Services

**Organization:** New York State Department of Financial Services (NYDFS)

**Source Type:** Government Investigation

**Title:** Twitter Investigation Report

**Published:** 14 October 2020

**URL:** https://www.dfs.ny.gov/reports-and-publications/other-reports/Twitter_Report

### What This Source Provides

- Detailed description of the social-engineering attack.
- Phishing website and fake Twitter VPN login.
- Employee credential compromise.
- MFA interaction.
- Initial access to Twitter's internal systems.
- Targeting of employees with access to internal support tools.
- Attack timeline.
- Quantitative impact information.
- Analysis of cybersecurity weaknesses.
- Recommendations for improving security awareness and phishing exercises.

### Importance to Our Project

This will be our primary source for reconstructing the technical attack chain and analyzing the cybersecurity weaknesses that contributed to the incident.

## Source 2 — Twitter Official Incident Disclosure

**Organization:** Twitter

**Source Type:** Official Company Disclosure

**Title:** An update on our security incident

**Published:** July 2020

**URL:** https://blog.x.com/en_us/topics/company/2020/an-update-on-our-security-incident

### What This Source Provides

- Twitter's official description of the incident.
- Confirmation of phone spear-phishing/social engineering.
- Employee credential compromise.
- Access to internal systems and support tools.
- Number of targeted and compromised accounts.
- Information potentially accessed.
- Twitter's immediate response.
- Security improvements following the incident.

### Importance to Our Project

This will be used as the organization's own account of the incident and to cross-check facts from the NYDFS investigation.

---

## Source Classification

### Source-Derived Facts

Information directly reported by NYDFS or Twitter will be treated as factual evidence.

### Our Analysis

Interpretations about why the attack succeeded or how security controls could have reduced the impact will be clearly identified as analysis.

### Recommendations

Security controls proposed by us will be presented separately from the documented incident facts.
