\# Impact Assessment



\## 1. Overview



The July 2020 Twitter incident affected Twitter's internal systems and a number of Twitter user accounts. The attackers used compromised employee access to reach internal account-support tools and subsequently compromise targeted accounts.



\---



\## 2. Quantitative Impact



Twitter reported the following figures:



| Metric | Reported Result |

|---|---:|

| Accounts targeted | 130 |

| Accounts used to send Tweets | 45 |

| Accounts with DM inbox accessed | 36 |

| Accounts from which Twitter data was downloaded | 7 |



The NYDFS investigation also reported that more than $118,000 worth of Bitcoin was transferred to attacker-controlled addresses.



These figures provide measurable evidence of the scale and consequences of the incident.



\---



\## 3. Confidentiality Impact



The confidentiality of affected accounts was compromised.



The attackers gained access to information associated with targeted accounts, including direct-message inboxes in 36 cases. Twitter also reported that data was downloaded from 7 accounts.



This demonstrates unauthorized access to information that should have remained accessible only to authorized users.



\---



\## 4. Integrity Impact



The integrity of affected Twitter accounts was compromised.



Attackers gained the ability to use 45 compromised accounts to publish Tweets. This allowed unauthorized content to be posted from accounts that users would normally trust.



The incident therefore affected the authenticity and integrity of communications made through those accounts.



\---



\## 5. Availability Impact



The available sources do not establish a significant long-term availability outage affecting Twitter's overall service.



However, Twitter restricted or locked affected accounts as part of its response, which temporarily affected access for some users.



Therefore, the primary impact was confidentiality and integrity rather than a major availability failure.



\---



\## 6. Financial Impact



The NYDFS investigation reported that more than $118,000 worth of Bitcoin was transferred to attacker-controlled addresses as part of the cryptocurrency fraud.



This demonstrates that the compromise resulted in direct financial consequences.



\---



\## 7. Operational Impact



Twitter had to respond to the compromise by:



\- Restricting affected accounts.

\- Investigating unauthorized activity.

\- Revoking or limiting compromised access.

\- Working with law enforcement and external investigators.

\- Restoring affected accounts.

\- Strengthening security controls.



The incident therefore required significant security and operational resources.



\---



\## 8. Reputational Impact



The attackers used compromised accounts belonging to prominent users and organizations to publish fraudulent cryptocurrency-related messages.



Because the messages originated from accounts that appeared legitimate, the incident had the potential to reduce user trust in the security of the platform.



This is an analytical assessment based on the nature of the compromise rather than a quantified measurement of reputational damage.



\---



\## 9. Risks to Affected Users



Affected users faced risks including:



\- Unauthorized access to account information.

\- Exposure of private communications in compromised accounts.

\- Impersonation using compromised accounts.

\- Financial loss from fraudulent cryptocurrency messages.

\- Potential follow-on social-engineering attacks.



The exact consequences varied depending on the account and information accessed.



\---



\## 10. CIA Triad Assessment



| Security Property | Impact | Reason |

|---|---|---|

| Confidentiality | High | Some DM inboxes and account data were accessed. |

| Integrity | High | Attackers used compromised accounts to publish unauthorized Tweets. |

| Availability | Low–Medium | Affected accounts were restricted during response, but a major platform-wide outage was not established by the sources. |



\---



\## 11. Overall Assessment



The incident demonstrates how compromise of a small number of employee accounts can develop into a much larger organizational security incident when those accounts provide access to privileged internal systems.



The most significant impacts were unauthorized access to information, manipulation of trusted accounts, financial fraud, and the operational effort required to contain and investigate the incident.



The quantitative figures reported by Twitter and NYDFS provide measurable evidence of the incident's scope.
